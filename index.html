CASE STUDY 01
GTM Launch · Defence & Energy

Turning edge video technology into pipeline

Videosoft's FireLight platform solves a problem that traditional video streaming cannot: delivering reliable, real-time video from as low as 1Kbps in degraded networks, remote locations, and mission-critical environments across energy, government, maritime, and aviation sectors. The buyer's challenge is operational: when communications infrastructure fails or bandwidth is constrained, teams lose situational awareness and the ability to make confident decisions. Videosoft keeps video flowing when every kilobit counts. The platform was technically exceptional but marketed around features rather than this mission-critical outcome. I repositioned the GTM narrative around operational continuity and real-time decision-making under pressure, using language that resonated directly with the operational buyers who approve these purchases, and led end-to-end launch execution that compressed the sales cycle and accelerated conversion.

CASE STUDY 02
Messaging Strategy · HR Tech SaaS
ISO 27001 Certified SOC 2 Type 2 Certified

From complex HR platform to boardroom outcomes

EnterpriseAlumni's platform helps enterprises unlock the commercial value of their alumni networks across three outcomes: accelerating recruitment through boomerang hires, generating new business pipeline through alumni referrals, and building brand advocacy at scale. The challenge was that buyers saw it as an HR administration tool rather than a revenue and talent accelerator. I rebuilt the messaging framework to reposition it as a strategic commercial asset, shifting the conversation from "managing former employees" to "activating a pre-vetted network that drives hiring, revenue, and brand growth." When AI-driven features were introduced, I also addressed buyer concerns around data security and AI reliability directly in the value narrative, building confidence without making compliance the centrepiece. The result was a GTM strategy that spoke to CHROs, CFOs, and business development leaders simultaneously.

CASE STUDY 03
Market Development · Fortune 500

Building the SMB printer market at Ricoh HK

Ricoh Hong Kong is a Fortune 500 multinational providing copying, printing, and IT solutions to enterprise and SMB customers. At the time, Ricoh's GTM focus was heavily weighted toward large enterprise accounts, leaving the SMB segment significantly underserved despite its commercial potential. I identified this white space through market analysis, built a targeted GTM strategy to capture it, developed segment-specific positioning and messaging that spoke to SMB buyers' priorities around cost efficiency and workflow productivity, launched new printer series with integrated campaign and channel strategies, and built a channel partnership programme from scratch to extend market reach. The result was sustained revenue growth at a Fortune 500 MNC through disciplined market development and channel execution.

PMM Perspective

Three things I believe about B2B GTM

After 20+ years leading GTM across different industries and markets, these are the convictions that shape every decision I make.

01

Most B2B companies build GTM around their product. The ones that win build it around their buyer's problem.

Feature-led messaging feels safe because it is easy to write. But buyers do not care what your product does. They care what it changes for them. The moment you reframe from capability to outcome, your pipeline accelerates. I have seen this hold true across HR Tech, AEC, IT research, and video technology. The sector changes. The buyer psychology does not.

02

PMM that does not sit at the product table is not product marketing. It is expensive copywriting.

The most common PMM failure I see is being brought in after the roadmap is locked. At that point you are packaging decisions made without market input. True PMM means customer insight shaping what gets built, not just how it gets described. When I joined EnterpriseAlumni, I built the customer advisory board structure precisely so buyer voice could reach the product team before sprint planning, not after launch.

03

Sales and marketing misalignment is never a communication problem. It is a positioning problem in disguise.

When sales ignores marketing collateral or rewrites the pitch, the instinct is to blame the relationship. The real issue is almost always that the positioning does not reflect how sales actually wins deals. The fix is not a better briefing session. It is going back to win-loss data, talking to the top performers, and rebuilding the messaging from what actually closes. Alignment follows clarity. It rarely precedes it.
