Why Clara

The interesting part isn't the AI. It's the trust.

Clara already handles 10,000+ calls a day for 2,000+ home-service businesses — real usage, not a beta — but the ambition is bigger: an AI workforce for owner-operators with no time for another piece of software. That's a different positioning problem than typical SaaS. HVAC, plumbing, and electrical owners aren't buying "AI" — they're buying "the phone gets answered and the job gets booked." The job here is translating category-level AI hype into category-specific trust.
That gap — between what's technically true and what a busy tradesperson actually believes — is the part of this role I find most interesting.
Day-1 hypothesis · outside-in, to validate

Understand → Hypothesis → Build → GTM → Observe → Learn

That's the loop in your job description. In that spirit, here's a first hypothesis built from public information only — exactly the kind of starting point I'd bring to a real segmentation sprint in week one, and exactly the kind I'd expect to be wrong about in places until I'm inside the data.

Segment

Not all "home-service SMBs" behave the same. A 1-person emergency plumber (unplannable, urgent, phone-is-the-business) has different call economics and different fear than a 5-person electrical firm running scheduled jobs. I'd bet positioning — and even packaging — should vary by call urgency profile, not just trade vertical.

Positioning hypothesis

The competitive frame isn't other AI tools — it's the status quo: missed calls, an answering service, or a family member covering the phone. Messaging that leads with "AI" competes with skepticism; messaging that leads with "every call becomes a booked job, even at 9pm" competes with the real alternative.

What I'd test first

Willingness to pay and trust threshold by segment — does a solo tradesperson trust and pay for this the same way a 5-person shop with an office manager does? That single question shapes pricing, onboarding, and messaging at once, and it's answerable fast with the win/loss and usage data Clara already has from 2,000+ live customers.

Stated plainly: I have zero visibility into Clara's actual funnel, churn, or segment performance. This is a hypothesis built the way I'd build one on day one — from the outside, cheap to be wrong about, and designed to be thrown out the moment real data disagrees with it.

Days 30–60 · Hypothesize & test

Put a stake in the ground

  • Publish a first segmentation and positioning point of view, and pressure-test it with Product, Growth, and Creative.

  • Ask real customers what they'd pay and why — same insight approach that shaped EnterpriseAlumni's roadmap, aimed at pricing this time.
Days 60–90 · Build & show evidence

Ship something measurable

  • Ship a living competitive battlecard for Sales, refreshed on the same AI-assisted cadence I ran at Videosoft — tied to measurable behavior, not launch-and-hope.
  • Report back on what the early experiments actually showed, and kill or double down accordingly.
  • Land on an evidence-backed point of view for where the next quarter's focus should go.
